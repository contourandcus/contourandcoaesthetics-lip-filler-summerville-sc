About the Treatment

What Lip Filler Actually Does, and What It Does Not

Lip filler is a smooth gel made from hyaluronic acid, a substance your body produces naturally. Placed precisely into the lip, it does three things at once: it restores structure where the lip has thinned, it refines the border and shape, and it attracts water, which is why treated lips usually feel softer and less dry, not just fuller.

What it does not do is change who you look like. The overfilled look that worries most first-time clients comes from too much product, placed too fast, in the wrong layer. That is a technique problem, not a product problem. Our approach is deliberately conservative: place a little, assess, adjust. You can always add more at a follow-up. Taking it away is a different conversation entirely, although hyaluronic acid fillers can be dissolved if ever needed, which is one reason we work with them.

We use lip-specific products, including options designed purely for the lips such as Revanesse Lips+, because lip tissue moves constantly and needs a softer, more flexible gel than the cheek or jawline does. Product choice is part of your plan, and we will explain it in plain language before anything is opened.

how much do I need?

Half a Syringe or a Full One? An Honest Guide

This is the question almost everyone asks, and it deserves a straighter answer than it usually gets. A full syringe of lip filler holds about a fifth of a teaspoon of gel. That is far less than most people picture, and spread across both lips it produces a visible but comfortable change for most faces.

A half syringe suits first-timers who want to restore definition and hydration with the subtlest possible shift, or clients maintaining a result they already love. A full syringe suits clients whose lips have noticeably thinned with time, or who want a clearer change in shape as well as softness. What we do not recommend is deciding based on price alone or on someone else’s before-and-after photos. Your tissue, your proportions and your goals decide, and that is exactly what your consultation is for.

Is It Right for You?

Who Makes a Good Candidate for Lip Filler

You are likely a good candidate if your lips have lost volume or definition over time, if your upper and lower lips feel out of proportion, if one side sits noticeably differently from the other, or if your lips simply never had the shape you wanted and you are ready to refine them in a way that still looks like you.

Good candidates share one trait that matters more than anatomy: realistic expectations. Lip filler refines and restores. It does not turn one person’s lips into another’s, and it works best a little at a time.

And we will ask you to wait if you have an active cold sore, a skin infection near the mouth, or certain medical situations we will screen for carefully at consultation. Waiting a few weeks is always better than treating at the wrong moment, and we would rather reschedule you than rush you.

What Your Appointment Looks Like, Start to Finish

Consultation first. We look at your lips together, in the mirror and in conversation, and talk about what bothers you and what you hope to see. If filler is not the right answer, we will tell you what is.

Lip Filler FAQs

Straight answers to the questions we hear most in our Summerville studio.

How long does lip filler last?

For most clients, lip filler lasts somewhere between 6 and 12 months. Lips move constantly, so they tend to break product down a little faster than quieter areas of the face. How long yours lasts depends on the product used, how much was placed, and your own metabolism. Most of our clients plan on a maintenance visit about once a year to keep their shape consistent, rather than letting it fade completely and starting over.

Does lip filler hurt?

Most clients are surprised by how manageable it is. We use a topical numbing cream before treatment, and most of the fillers we use for lips also contain lidocaine, so comfort improves as we go. You will feel pressure and a few brief pinches, and the lips can feel tender and look swollen for a couple of days afterwards. Genuine sharp pain is not expected, and we will always tell you exactly what is happening as we work.

How much lip filler do I need for my first time?

For most first-time clients, a single syringe, or even half of one, is plenty. A full syringe sounds like a lot, but it is only about a fifth of a teaspoon of gel. If you want a subtle refresh rather than a visible size change, starting with half a syringe is a sensible, low-commitment way to see how your lips respond. Your consultation is where we look at your natural shape and proportions together and agree on an amount before anything is opened.

What is the downtime after lip filler?

There is no formal downtime, and most people go straight back to work or errands. Swelling is the main thing to plan around: lips usually look their fullest on day two, settle noticeably by day four or five, and show their true result at about two weeks. Bruising is possible, so we suggest scheduling at least two weeks before a wedding, photos, or any event where you want to look your best.

Will my lips look natural, or will people know I had filler?

Done conservatively, lip filler should read as your lips on a good day, not as a procedure. The obvious, overfilled look comes from too much product, placed too quickly, in the wrong layer. Our approach is to place a little, assess, and adjust, working with your own lip border and proportions rather than redrawing them. Most clients tell us people notice they look refreshed without being able to say why.

Can lip filler be removed if I do not like it?

Yes. The hyaluronic acid fillers we use for lips can be dissolved with an enzyme called hyaluronidase if you are ever unhappy with a result or simply want to start again. That reversibility is one of the reasons we work with this class of product. Our goal is always to plan carefully enough upfront that you never need it.
